# W3 · G3 Demo moment

1. **Before → after.** Before: dev blog on static site + giscus/Disqus. Comments arrive as spam or bot noise, the author cleans them up by hand, and reader fixes need a GitHub PR (E331, E304, E005, E311, E338). After: reader highlights a line and suggests a fix, spam gets filtered automatically, the author clicks approve, and the post shows a revision diff with "fixed by @reader" credit.
   Result: line written. Pain side is backed by spam/moderation quotes, but there are only about 3 recent first-person quotes (E331, E304, E305).
2. **Visible?** Yes: inline highlight, a spam item sent to the filtered pile, a diff, and a credit badge all show on screen.
   Result: pass.
3. **≤2 min / ≤5 clicks?** Path: select text → suggest → (seeded spam shows as filtered) → approve → view revised post. That is 4-5 clicks and about 60-90s with seeded data.
   Result: pass.
4. **Generic AI wrapper risk?** Medium. The spam filter is the only AI part, and it is a commodity: Akismet exists, and WordPress/Remark42 already do moderation (research/analysis_comparison.csv). Inline annotations exist too (Hypothesis, Medium highlights), and PR-based reader fixes are what MDN does (E338). The new part is suggestion → approved revision → credit. It is visible but incremental.
   Result: partial.

**Strongest wow the evidence supports:** split screen. On the left, 10 seeded incoming items (7 spam/bot like E304, 3 real fixes), and the filter sorts them live. On the right, the author approves one real fix with one click, and the published post updates with a diff and a contributor credit. The spam → clean queue → credited revision happens in a single take, under 60s.

**Mark: PARTIAL.** The before → after is visible and fast. The wow is underwhelming because spam filtering and inline comments are commodity features, and no evidence shows that authors want reader edits merged into revisions.

**Missing evidence:**
- Recent first-person dev-author quotes saying they would accept or merge reader suggestions (only E338 MDN, which is an institution, not a personal blog).
- Volume/time numbers for spam moderation on personal dev blogs (E331 is qualitative).
- Proof that the AI filter beats Akismet/giscus (GitHub auth) on spam, or else the wow is not differentiated.
- refs/producthunt.md: no comparable "reader-suggested revision" launch was checked for a reaction signal.
